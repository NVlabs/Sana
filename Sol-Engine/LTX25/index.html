Day One Optimization

Accelerating LTX-2.5 with Sol Engineachieving a 3.52× end-to-end speedup

A 22B audio-video diffusion transformer, generating 1536×1024 at 24 fps with synchronized sound. On 2× NVIDIA GB200, the Sol Video Inference Engine cuts warm end-to-end latency from 41.59 seconds to 11.81 seconds by combining CFG parallelism, First Block Cache, and compiled execution — no distillation, no LoRA, and no change to the released schedule.

Yitong Li*, Haopeng Li*, Junsong Chen, Jincheng Yu, Enze Xie NVIDIA SANA Team August 13, 2026
01 — LTX-2.5

One transformer, two stages, picture and sound in the same pass.

LTX-2.5 is Lightricks' 22B diffusion transformer for generating video and synchronized audio together. Its released inference package combines the 22B DiT with a convolutional video VAE, an audio VAE, and a Gemma text encoder. For the 1536×1024 benchmark configuration used in this article, the pipeline runs a 30-step guided first stage at 768×512, upsamples the latent representation, and finishes with a two-step distilled stage at 1536×1024.

02 — Sol Engine

3.52× end-to-end speedup

Measured on 2× NVIDIA GB200 · warm end-to-end wall time.

The complete Sol Engine recipe reaches 11.81 seconds, down from 41.59 seconds for the official out-of-the-box baseline. That 3.52× cumulative gain comes from stacking CFG parallelism, First Block Cache, and compiled execution against an unchanged checkpoint and sampler.

Where the speedup comes from

The gains stack layer by layer. CFG parallelism removes frequent communication overhead in multi-GPU inference, First Block Cache skips redundant denoiser work, and compilation fuses the remaining execution path. Together they reduce warm end-to-end latency from 41.59 seconds to 11.81 seconds.

CFG parallelism. Stage 1 runs at 768×512 in this configuration, so its token sequence is relatively short. Sequence parallelism therefore spends a large share of runtime on communication and falls well short of the ideal 2.00× scaling over one GPU. We instead split the four guidance passes across two CFG ranks, with each rank handling half of the guidance workload. This requires much less communication and scales more efficiently for this workload.

First Block Cache. It detects denoising steps whose outputs would change very little and reuses the previous result instead of running another full denoiser forward pass. This reduces the total number of forwards while keeping the overall visual result close to the uncached reference. At the 0.08 threshold used here, it skips 9 of 30 steps.

Cache-aware compilation. Each of the 48 transformer blocks is compiled separately with torch.compile, while FBCache control remains in eager Python. Every step runs the compiled Block 0, measures its residual change, and synchronizes the decision across both CFG ranks with all_reduce(MIN). A cache hit reuses the previous residual and skips Blocks 1–47; a miss executes the remaining compiled blocks. Because compilation is lazy, tracing and kernel generation are treated as warm-up, and the reported latency uses only requests 6–10.

03 — Sol Engine

Agent-Native Auto Optimization

Three kinds of specificity separate a published method from a deployed model.

The community already has the toolbox — sparse attention, diffusion caching, kernel fusion — and every method comes with convincing numbers. It also comes with knobs. Any real deployment fixes three things at once: a specific model, specific hardware, and a specific configuration of duration, frame rate, and resolution. A setting tuned under one combination rarely holds under another, so recipes do not transfer directly and each deployment has to be tuned case by case — which is why accelerating a new model has historically taken weeks of hand-tuning rather than an afternoon.

Sol Engine closes that gap by search instead of by hand. It runs one optimization search per technique family, concurrently — kernel, sparse attention, cache — each sweeping its own knobs against the real checkpoint on the real GPUs. The results cannot simply be concatenated, because the techniques interact: caching changes which steps sparse attention has to serve. So the surviving candidates go to an integrator that searches over combinations rather than over knobs, with a human validator signing off on the output video.
