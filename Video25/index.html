00Abstract

We introduce SANA-Video 2.5, a 16B model that generates 5–15 s 768p videos with synchronised sound in seconds on a single GPU. Linear attention keeps a fixed-size state at any length, suiting long-horizon generation. SANA-Video 2.5 first scales the hybrid linear-attention backbone of SANA-Video 2.0 to 16B parameters and 15 s clips with sound. Core designs include:

01

Parallel audio stream

A 2B audio stream exchanges information with every layer of a 14B hybrid video model through time-aligned cross-attention.

02

Staged pre-training

Audio is bootstrapped beside the frozen, LoRA-adapted video weights, then all 16B parameters train jointly.

03

Cross-latent few-step distillation

4- and 8-step students learn from the stronger 33B MiniMax-H3 in its own latent space, and a Gauss–Newton pullback through a learned adapter raises the realised cosine of the teacher's update from 0.33 to 0.88.

On one GB200, the 4-step student generates a 5 s 768p clip with audio in 2.9 s, 44× faster than the 50-step teacher and 6–7× faster than few-step H3 models, yet scores higher than both on HPSv3. The 8-step student generates a 15 s clip in 14.7 s, faster than 8-step LTX-2.5 distilled (20.8 s), and in 9.0 s with Sol-Engine, MXFP8 and a tiny VAE decoder. Fine-tuned with read-only reference banks, the model further generates video and sound from multiple reference images, clips and sounds.

01Results

Video and sound, generated together

The 8-step 14B student at 1344×768 and 24 fps. Every clip carries its own soundtrack, with speech, music and effects generated together with the frames. Turn the sound on, and click a video to read its prompt.

02Method

Sound and speed, built around an unchanged hybrid backbone

The 14B video stream keeps the 3 : 1 linear-to-softmax layout of SANA-Video 2.0, its kernels and its RoPE. Everything new is added around it.

01

A parallel audio stream

A 40-layer, 2B audio stream runs beside the video stream. In every layer the two read each other through cross-attention aligned in physical time. Of four interaction designs compared with matched data and training steps, it gives the best text–audio agreement and is the only one that leaves the video stream's linear and softmax layers untouched, so the joint model keeps the speed of the video model.

  • 40 layers in each stream
  • 3 : 1 linear to softmax in video
  • +0.073 CLAP over the baseline
02

Staged audio–video pre-training

A randomly initialised audio stream sends noise-like gradients into a trained video model. Audio is therefore grown next to the frozen video model and coupled through a low-rank adapter before all 16B parameters train jointly with per-stream learning rates, so the video prior never trains against an untrained partner.

03

Distillation from a stronger teacher in another latent space

The 40-step model is distilled into 4- and 8-step students by DMD from MiniMax-H3, a 33B teacher in a different latent space, through a learned latent adapter. Backpropagation through the adapter keeps only a cosine of 0.33 of the teacher's update (0.22 for the 5B testbed), which collapses into a global colour shift. A damped Gauss–Newton pullback solves for the student move that the adapter maps onto the update instead.
