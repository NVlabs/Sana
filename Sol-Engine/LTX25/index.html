01 — LTX-2.5

One transformer, two stages, picture and sound in the same pass.

LTX-2.5 is Lightricks' 22B diffusion transformer for generating video and synchronized audio together. Its released inference package combines the 22B DiT with a convolutional video VAE, an audio VAE, and a Gemma text encoder. For the 1536×1024 benchmark configuration used in this article, the pipeline runs a 30-step guided first stage at 768×512, upsamples the latent representation, and finishes with a two-step distilled stage at 1536×1024.

The Distilled Pipeline is designed for fast, efficient inference: its eight-step first stage and CFG-free execution reduce iterative denoising, while leaving less refinement headroom for motion, structure, and fine detail. The Multi-step Pipeline takes the complementary approach, using 30 guided first-stage steps to progressively resolve the generation and prioritize higher video quality at greater compute cost. These two operating points naturally lead to two optimization paths: Section 3 optimizes the Multi-step BF16 pipeline end to end on 4× B200, while Section 4 focuses on accelerating the Distilled pipeline on a single GPU.

03 — Multi-step Pipeline Optimization

To accelerate video generation without changing the Multi-step Pipeline's high-quality operating point, we optimize the full inference stack at three complementary levels.

  1. Diffusion Cache. The Multi-step Pipeline executes 30 steps in Stage 1, leaving redundancy across adjacent diffusion-denoising steps. We apply First Block Cache to reuse stable residuals and skip unnecessary forwards, delivering nearly 50% higher Stage 1 performance while preserving the overall visual result.
  2. Parallelism Optimization. Because the first-stage token sequence is relatively compact, sequence parallelism leaves too much communication overhead. At the same time, the high-quality Multi-step Pipeline contains four CFG branches, which naturally allows the guidance workload to be distributed across four GPUs with substantially less communication.
  3. Kernel optimization. Cache-aware compilation and kernel fusion reduce launch overhead and avoid unnecessary memory traffic. Block 0 remains the cache decision point, while cache misses execute the remaining compiled transformer blocks.

04 — Distilled Pipeline Optimization

Our setting uses the NVFP4 Distilled Pipeline, with an 8-step Stage 1 and a 3-step Stage 2. Stage 1 operates on a relatively compact token sequence, and its denoising schedule is already well optimized; consequently, our focus shifts to Stage 2. At 4K for five seconds, attention becomes the dominant cost in this higher-resolution refinement stage. We therefore apply Sol-Attn, a training-free sparse attention method, to target this bottleneck without additional training or calibration.

Stage 2 Optimization — Core Technology: Sol-Attn

At high resolution and long duration, with audio tokens interleaved into the same attention, sequence length dominates the cost. Attention maps in video diffusion are also extremely sparse: most query blocks attend meaningfully to only a small fraction of key blocks. This structure calls for an efficient sparse attention method that reduces computation without a catastrophic loss in quality.

Sol-Attn is training-free and calibration-free. It folds block selection into the online-softmax pass itself, avoiding a separate routing stage:

  • Query-dependent thresholding. Instead of a fixed top-k or top-p, each query row derives a threshold from its block proxy-score statistics, τᵢ = μᵢ + βσᵢ. One β value controls density across layers, steps, and resolutions without learning or offline calibration.
  • On-the-fly routing. Pooled keys stream chunk-wise and are compared with the threshold during softmax accumulation. No proxy map is materialized, keeping routing overhead low at long sequence lengths.
  • Proxy-score reuse. Rejected blocks are approximated with a Taylor expansion around the row-wise mean score, recovering probability mass that hard selection discards and preserving quality at high sparsity.

05 — Sol Engine

Agent-Native Auto Optimization

Three kinds of specificity separate a published method from a deployed model.

We use Sol Engine to optimize video inference across different hardware and configurations:

  • Hardware: NVIDIA DGX Spark, B200, and other GPU platforms.
  • Configurations: different resolutions and durations, including 4K and 1080p workloads.

For each deployment target, Sol Engine combines:

  • Parallelism optimization
  • Diffusion cache
  • Kernel fusion
  • Sparse attention

Sol Engine closes that gap by search instead of by hand. It runs one optimization search per technique family, concurrently — kernel, sparse attention, cache — each sweeping its own knobs against the real checkpoint on the real GPUs. The results cannot simply be concatenated, because the techniques interact: caching changes which steps sparse attention has to serve. So the surviving candidates go to an integrator that searches over combinations rather than over knobs, with a human validator signing off on the output video.
